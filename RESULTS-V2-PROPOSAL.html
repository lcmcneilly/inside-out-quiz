Showing the four

Why no percentages. Three of the four patterns rest on a single question, so each has only three possible states. Judgement has five. A percentage would read as precision the quiz cannot actually deliver, and the first person to get 33% would reasonably ask what the other 67% was.

Option one · words only

JudgementLoud
FearPresent
NeedinessQuiet
JealousyNot in your answers

Four words doing four jobs. Nothing implies measurement, which is honest about what eight questions can tell anyone. The quietest option visually, and the one that sits best beside type this large.

Option two · words and a line

JudgementLoud
FearPresent
NeedinessQuiet
JealousyNot in your answers

The line is 2px, the same weight as the gold rule elsewhere, and gold only on the leader. Scannable in a second. The risk is the one above: a line invites people to compare lengths precisely, which these numbers do not support.

Option three · ranked, no magnitude

01Judgement
How fast you reach a verdict
02Fear
The reasons you give
03Neediness
How much of your steadiness someone else holds
04Jealousy
Where you stand when you measure

Order only, no levels at all, and each one explains what it was looking at. Most informative and the best teaching, but a strict ranking claims that fourth is definitely quieter than third, which a one-point gap does not really earn.

My pick is option one, with the four plain-English descriptions from option three folded in underneath each name. It makes no claim the quiz cannot back, it reads at a glance, and it explains what was being looked at without naming the machinery.
