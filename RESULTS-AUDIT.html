How the results are generated

Every answer, every result, and what I found by running all 6,561 possible ways through the quiz against the live scoring code.

The mechanism, in order

1 · Four territories. Five of the eight questions score. Each belongs to one of four territories, which become the four results. The territory names are internal and never appear on screen.

TerritoryResultQuestionsMax
verdictJudgement24
measuringJealousy12

2 · Each answer carries a weight of 2, 1 or 0. Two is the loud end, zero is settled.

3 · Scored as a share of its own maximum, not as a raw total. Judgement has two questions behind it, so a raw total would hand it the result almost every time.

4 · The highest share names the result. This is where the first defect lives.

5 · The quote block reads back the answer that caused it, in the person's own words. If no answer sat at the far end there is nothing to quote, so a stand-in line appears instead. This is where the second defect lives.

6 · The door. SHE IS SOL unless two things both hold: no answer at the far end anywhere, and all three AI answers at the strong end.

Defect one · the contradiction you hit

864 of 6,561 routes — 13.2% — print a pattern name in large type and then say underneath it that none of the four ran loud. Those are two opposite claims on one screen. It happens whenever the leading territory has no answer at the far end, which a single middling answer is enough to cause.

Defect two · ties always go to Jealousy

When two territories score identically, the winner is decided by the order they happen to sit in the code, not by anything meaningful. Of 810 tied cases, Jealousy won 810 and Neediness won none. Answer loudly on three different patterns and you will be told it is Jealousy every time.

Defect three · Jealousy is over-represented

34.6% against an even quarter. Partly defect two, partly that one question can reach a full score while Judgement needs both of its questions at the far end. "Nothing much" scoring a middle point means indifference alone can name Jealousy.

The three AI questions

These do not affect which pattern you get. They decide the door, and all three must be at the strong end for SHE IS AI.

  • Before you ask AI something, how clear are you on what you actually want? — strong answer: Clear first, then I ask
  • When you use AI on something that matters, whose thinking ends up in the finished thing? — strong answer: Mostly mine. It speeds up work I had already decided on
  • When AI gives you an answer and something in you says no, what usually happens? — strong answer: I trust the no, and argue with it
