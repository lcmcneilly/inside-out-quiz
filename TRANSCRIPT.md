## 5 · PART ONE · THE FOUR

### What moves through you

This section is based on emotional elements — four of them, and they are the four that do the most damage when nobody is watching them: judgement, jealousy, neediness and fear.

#### Why these four

They rarely announce themselves. Each one arrives dressed as something reasonable — judgement as discernment, jealousy as ambition, neediness as closeness, fear as good sense. Left unwatched they don't take a life apart in one blow; they make a thousand small decisions on your behalf until you can't tell which of them were yours.

Each has a counter: compassion, joy, love, empathy. Not nicer feelings to have instead — the specific things that take each one apart. The questions below simply ask how often each shows up.

*Scale on every question: Almost never · Sometimes · Often · Almost always*

**Judgement** — counter: Compassion

1. I reach a verdict on someone before I know what they're carrying.
2. What I can't tolerate in someone else turns out to be something I don't allow in myself.
3. When someone behaves badly, I look for the reason before I look for the fault.  *(reverse-scored)*
4. I run a quiet commentary on how I'm doing against how I should be doing.

**Jealousy** — counter: Joy

5. When someone gets the thing I want, something in me goes quiet rather than glad.
6. I measure how far along I am against people who started somewhere different from me.
7. I avoid looking at what someone else is doing because of how it makes me feel.
8. I can be pleased for someone whose success shows up what I haven't done yet.  *(reverse-scored)*

**Neediness** — counter: Love

9. I ask for reassurance more than the situation actually calls for.
10. How settled I feel depends on how somebody responded to me that day.
11. I give in ways that quietly expect something back.
12. I can sit with someone's silence without deciding what it means about me.  *(reverse-scored)*

**Fear** — counter: Empathy

13. Fear talks me out of things I actually want.
14. I call something "being realistic" when I know underneath that it's fear.
15. I brace for losing something I don't yet fully have.
16. I can take a step before the fear has finished talking.  *(reverse-scored)*

17. Of those four, which was the hardest to answer honestly?

## 6 · PART TWO · THE THREE

### What you are built on

The Pyramid of Creation is the model underneath everything taught here. It says that anything you build in your life rests on three layers, in a fixed order, and that a thing only holds if the layer under it is sound.

#### The three layers

Trust and belief are the ground — whether your own word means anything to you, and what you have quietly agreed is true about yourself. Emotion is the energy — the fuel that actually moves something out of your head and into the world. Thought and intent are the blueprint — the shape you draw and the direction you hold.

Almost everyone works the blueprint, because it is the visible one, and then can't understand why nothing stands up. These questions look at all three.

**the ground** — trust and belief

18. I keep the promises I make to myself.
19. I trust my own read on something rather than second-guessing it.
20. I catch myself living by a belief I never actually chose.  *(reverse-scored)*

**the energy** — emotion

21. I can tell, in the moment, whether I'm moving from fear or from love.
22. I let a feeling move through me rather than storing it somewhere.
23. I carry a feeling for weeks without knowing what started it.  *(reverse-scored)*

**the blueprint** — thought and intent

24. I can watch a thought without believing it.
25. An intention I set in the morning is still shaping what I do by the afternoon.
26. I treat my inner state as the cause of what happens rather than the result of it.

## 7 · PART THREE · THE TWO

### What the whole thing runs on

Two disciplines, and everything else depends on them. One is the line between a life that happens to you and one you are the author of. The other is what makes crossing it possible.

**Self-responsibility**

Not guilt, and not blame turned inward. Simply where authorship sits — the difference between a life that happens to you and one you are the author of.

27. When something goes wrong, my first move is to look at my own part in it.
28. I hold a person or a circumstance responsible for how I feel.  *(reverse-scored)*

**Self-awareness**

The gap between what happens and how you respond. You are not your reactions — you are the one who can watch them arrive.

29. I notice a reaction rising before it gets out of my mouth.
30. I can name what I'm feeling while I'm still feeling it.

## 8 · PART FOUR · THE ONE

### Source

Three questions sit underneath everything here: who are you, what are you, where are you. Nobody answers them once. These six read where you're standing today — slide to wherever is honest, and don't tidy it up on the way.

*Scale: 1 to 9, between two named ends*

    *1 = almost none of it  →  9 = nearly all of it*
